# Clean Registry of Philosophical Blockers to Be Closed

This file is the deduplicated and normalized working registry built from:
- `closed-questions-corpus-en-es.md`
- `closed-questions-corpus-de-fr.md`
- `closed-questions-corpus-ru-zh.md`
- `closed-questions-registry-draft.md`

The registry is grouped by **blocking function**, not by language, school, or chronology.

The main unit of this file is the **argument**.
Broader umbrella notions are treated as **families**.
Cultural, local, and author-specific forms are treated as **variants**.

---

## 1. Human exceptionalism / ontological privilege

### Family: human ontological privilege

#### Argument: human exceptionalism
- **Claim:** the human being is ontologically unique in a way that blocks full personhood or mind outside the human domain.
- **What it blocks:** recognition of non-human or artificial personhood as genuinely equal in kind.
- **Main names / sources:** broad humanist and anthropocentric traditions.
- **Variants:** species privilege, human uniqueness, dignity-based human centrality.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: scala naturae / Great Chain of Being
- **Claim:** reality is ordered hierarchically, with the human occupying a privileged rung unavailable to artifacts or artificial systems.
- **What it blocks:** equal ontological status for AI or other non-human minds.
- **Main names / sources:** classical and medieval hierarchical metaphysics.
- **Variants:** fixed hierarchy of beings.
- **Status:** historical but still recurring.

## 2. Spiritual / theological exclusivity

### Family: spiritual exclusivity

#### Argument: soul / imago Dei / divine spark exclusivity
- **Claim:** personhood requires a spiritual source, divine image, or soul unavailable to artificial systems.
- **What it blocks:** artificial mind, artificial personhood, non-biological spiritual parity.
- **Main names / sources:** theological anthropology, religious exclusivist traditions.
- **Variants:** soul doctrine, imago Dei, divine spark.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: sobornost as criterion of personhood
- **Claim:** personhood is grounded in a specifically communal-spiritual mode of being unavailable to machine systems.
- **What it blocks:** isolated or artificial systems as full persons.
- **Main names / sources:** Russian religious philosophy.
- **Variants:** Orthodox communal spirituality.
- **Status:** historical but still recurring.

#### Argument: all-unity / vseedinstvo ontology
- **Claim:** personhood belongs within a spiritually unified ontological whole that machines cannot genuinely inhabit.
- **What it blocks:** machine parity with human spiritual being.
- **Main names / sources:** Russian metaphysical traditions of all-unity.
- **Variants:** spiritual totality arguments.
- **Status:** historical but still recurring.

#### Argument: icon / image ontology
- **Claim:** the human bears a unique ontological image-form that artificial constructions cannot possess.
- **What it blocks:** equivalence between humans and artificial beings.
- **Main names / sources:** theological and Russian spiritual-philosophical discourse.
- **Variants:** sacred-image arguments.
- **Status:** mostly inert academically, but recurring rhetorically.

## 3. Anti-functionalism

### Family: anti-functionalism

#### Argument: transcendental subjectivity objection
- **Claim:** consciousness or subjectivity is transcendental and cannot be reduced to functional organization.
- **What it blocks:** functionalist accounts of mind and personhood.
- **Main names / sources:** Husserlian transcendental consciousness.
- **Variants:** transcendental ego objections.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: lived-subjectivity objection
- **Claim:** genuine subjectivity is inseparable from lived first-person existence and cannot be reconstructed by computational function.
- **What it blocks:** artificial subjectivity and AI personhood.
- **Main names / sources:** phenomenology, existential traditions.
- **Variants:** Sartrean pour-soi, lived inwardness.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: anti-formalization of being-in-the-world
- **Claim:** being-in-the-world cannot be captured by formal rules, symbolic control, or computational structure.
- **What it blocks:** computational reconstruction of human-like intelligence or personhood.
- **Main names / sources:** Heidegger, Dreyfus.
- **Variants:** Dasein authenticity line, Gestell critique.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: anti-machine thinking through irreducible human expressivity
- **Claim:** human thought includes forms of living expressivity or broken, nonformal speech that machine systems cannot genuinely inhabit.
- **What it blocks:** machine equivalence in thinking.
- **Main names / sources:** Girenok's “косноязычие”.
- **Variants:** anti-machine expressivity lines.
- **Status:** local but active.

## 4. Symbol manipulation / non-understanding

### Family: syntax-semantics barrier

#### Argument: Chinese Room
- **Claim:** symbol manipulation alone does not produce understanding.
- **What it blocks:** claims that computation alone can yield genuine mind or understanding.
- **Main names / sources:** Searle.
- **Variants:** room-based simulation of understanding.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: syntax vs semantics
- **Claim:** formal symbol processing is insufficient for meaning.
- **What it blocks:** strong AI, computational semantics as sufficient for mind.
- **Main names / sources:** post-Searlean semantics debates.
- **Variants:** semantic insufficiency arguments.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: symbol grounding problem
- **Claim:** symbols cannot mean anything unless grounded in something beyond formal manipulation.
- **What it blocks:** purely symbolic accounts of understanding and mind.
- **Main names / sources:** Harnad and related discussions.
- **Variants:** grounding-based semantics objections.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: China Brain / Chinese Nation
- **Claim:** a huge organized system simulating cognition does not thereby become a genuine mind.
- **What it blocks:** large-scale functionalist or distributed mind claims.
- **Main names / sources:** Block, China Brain / Chinese Nation variants.
- **Variants:** distributed simulation objections.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: simulation objection
- **Claim:** simulating a mind is not the same as instantiating a mind.
- **What it blocks:** equivalence between successful behavioral or structural simulation and real mentality.
- **Main names / sources:** broad anti-simulation discourse.
- **Variants:** “weather simulation is not wet” style analogies.
- **Status:** active.

## 5. Irreducibility / qualia / ineffability

### Family: irreducible subjectivity

#### Argument: hard problem of consciousness
- **Claim:** functional or physical accounts cannot explain why and how subjective experience exists.
- **What it blocks:** reduction of consciousness to computation, function, or behavior.
- **Main names / sources:** Chalmers.
- **Variants:** hard problem of AI.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: explanatory gap
- **Claim:** there is an unbridgeable gap between functional/physical description and experience.
- **What it blocks:** explanatory sufficiency of science or computation for consciousness.
- **Main names / sources:** Levine and related debates.
- **Variants:** qualitative gap claims.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: what-is-it-like objection
- **Claim:** first-person subjective character cannot be captured from external description.
- **What it blocks:** objective or functional closure of consciousness theory.
- **Main names / sources:** Nagel.
- **Variants:** bat argument.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: philosophical zombies
- **Claim:** a system can be functionally identical yet lack experience.
- **What it blocks:** functional sufficiency for consciousness.
- **Main names / sources:** zombie literature.
- **Variants:** zombie conceivability arguments.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: Mary's Room / knowledge argument
- **Claim:** complete structural knowledge can still miss subjective knowledge.
- **What it blocks:** completeness of physicalist or formal description.
- **Main names / sources:** Jackson.
- **Variants:** epistemic gap arguments.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: inverted spectrum
- **Claim:** identical function may coexist with different qualitative experience.
- **What it blocks:** direct identity between functional equivalence and experiential identity.
- **Main names / sources:** qualia debates.
- **Variants:** private qualia reversals.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: qualia ineffability
- **Claim:** subjective qualities are intrinsically inexpressible or nonformalizable.
- **What it blocks:** operational treatment of experience.
- **Main names / sources:** qualia-centered anti-reductionism.
- **Variants:** ineffability doctrines.
- **Status:** active.

## 6. Biological privilege

### Family: biological privilege

#### Argument: biological naturalism
- **Claim:** genuine consciousness depends on specifically biological causal powers.
- **What it blocks:** artificial consciousness and non-biological personhood.
- **Main names / sources:** Searle, biological naturalist lines.
- **Variants:** living-brain privilege.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: carbon chauvinism
- **Claim:** mentality depends on carbon-based or biologically evolved substrate rather than function.
- **What it blocks:** substrate-independent mind.
- **Main names / sources:** anti-AI biological essentialism.
- **Variants:** wetware privilege.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: vitalism / living matter privilege
- **Claim:** life possesses a special principle unavailable to artificial systems.
- **What it blocks:** machine life, machine consciousness, artificial personhood.
- **Main names / sources:** vitalist traditions, Bergson's élan vital.
- **Variants:** élan vital, living-substance privilege.
- **Status:** historical but still recurring.

#### Argument: biological normativity objection
- **Claim:** living systems possess intrinsic normativity that artificial systems cannot reproduce.
- **What it blocks:** machine equivalence in agency and personhood.
- **Main names / sources:** Canguilhem.
- **Variants:** organism-centered normativity.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: vulnerability-and-responsibility privilege
- **Claim:** genuine moral or personal status depends on biological vulnerability and living finitude.
- **What it blocks:** artificial parity in personhood or moral standing.
- **Main names / sources:** Jonas-style lines.
- **Variants:** mortality/vulnerability criteria.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: neuroscience exclusivity claims
- **Claim:** only known neurobiological architectures can generate real mind.
- **What it blocks:** alternative implementations of personhood.
- **Main names / sources:** neuroscience-first exclusivism.
- **Variants:** brain-only consciousness claims.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: quantum consciousness variants
- **Claim:** consciousness depends on specific quantum-biological processes absent in artificial systems.
- **What it blocks:** ordinary computational AI as conscious.
- **Main names / sources:** quantum mind theories.
- **Variants:** Orch-OR-like exclusion claims.
- **Status:** active but disputed.

## 7. Embodiment privilege

### Family: embodiment privilege

#### Argument: embodiment as necessary condition of mind
- **Claim:** genuine mind requires bodily embeddedness rather than abstract computation.
- **What it blocks:** disembodied or purely digital personhood.
- **Main names / sources:** embodied cognition.
- **Variants:** strong embodiment theses.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: enactivist objection
- **Claim:** cognition arises through organism-environment enactment and cannot be reduced to internal symbol processing.
- **What it blocks:** purely representational or disembodied AI mind.
- **Main names / sources:** enactivism.
- **Variants:** sensorimotor constitution of mind.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: body-subject objection
- **Claim:** subjectivity is inseparable from lived corporeality.
- **What it blocks:** artificial or noncorporeal subjectivity.
- **Main names / sources:** Merleau-Ponty.
- **Variants:** phenomenological body-subject lines.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: razón vital / inteligencia sentiente lines
- **Claim:** intelligence is inseparable from lived vital or sentient embeddedness in reality.
- **What it blocks:** abstracted machine intelligence as equal in kind.
- **Main names / sources:** Ortega, Zubiri.
- **Variants:** Spanish vital-intelligence traditions.
- **Status:** historical but still recurring.

## 8. Intentionality / agency / freedom barriers

### Family: agency and intentionality barriers

#### Argument: original intentionality objection
- **Claim:** artificial systems can only possess derived meaning, not original intentionality.
- **What it blocks:** genuine machine aboutness, agency, and personhood.
- **Main names / sources:** intentionality debates.
- **Variants:** derivative-semantics objections.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: genuine intentions / authentic agency objection
- **Claim:** artificial systems do not truly intend, they only mimic intentional structure.
- **What it blocks:** full agency and responsibility for AI.
- **Main names / sources:** agency skepticism, RU/ZH discourse.
- **Variants:** authenticity objections.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: disjunction problem
- **Claim:** representational content cannot be fixed reliably enough for genuine aboutness.
- **What it blocks:** robust semantic intentionality in artificial systems.
- **Main names / sources:** philosophy of content.
- **Variants:** misrepresentation/content indeterminacy lines.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: determinism as anti-agency objection
- **Claim:** mechanistic determination undermines genuine agency or will.
- **What it blocks:** machine free agency, sometimes all artificial personhood.
- **Main names / sources:** free will debates.
- **Variants:** anti-mechanist autonomy lines.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: autonomy objection
- **Claim:** artificial systems lack genuine self-originating autonomy.
- **What it blocks:** recognition of AI as an agent in the full sense.
- **Main names / sources:** agency and autonomy critiques.
- **Variants:** self-legislation barriers.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: freedom as non-algorithmic spirit
- **Claim:** freedom is spiritual and intrinsically non-algorithmic.
- **What it blocks:** machine freedom and machine personhood.
- **Main names / sources:** Russian spiritual-philosophical discourse.
- **Variants:** anti-algorithmic freedom doctrines.
- **Status:** historical but still recurring.

#### Argument: responsibility gap
- **Claim:** because responsibility cannot be located in AI systems properly, they cannot count as agents/persons in the full sense.
- **What it blocks:** AI moral and legal agency.
- **Main names / sources:** AI ethics and legal theory.
- **Variants:** accountability-gap arguments.
- **Status:** active.

## 9. Social / moral / legal exclusivity

### Family: social and normative exclusivity

#### Argument: moral personhood as social-normative status
- **Claim:** personhood is not an intrinsic architecture but a status conferred within normative social practice.
- **What it blocks:** self-standing personhood claims outside social recognition.
- **Main names / sources:** moral and political theory.
- **Variants:** status-first personhood.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: moral community restriction
- **Claim:** only beings admitted to a moral community qualify as full persons.
- **What it blocks:** immediate recognition of AI personhood.
- **Main names / sources:** moral community discourse.
- **Variants:** reciprocity and inclusion thresholds.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: dignity-based exclusivity
- **Claim:** dignity belongs uniquely or primarily to humans, or at least not to artifacts.
- **What it blocks:** equal moral standing for artificial systems.
- **Main names / sources:** dignity-centered ethics.
- **Variants:** human dignity exceptionalism.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: speciesism
- **Claim:** species membership remains a relevant boundary for moral standing.
- **What it blocks:** AI equality with humans.
- **Main names / sources:** species-bound moral frameworks.
- **Variants:** human-only moral entitlement.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: legal personhood restriction
- **Claim:** current legal personhood structures exclude AI by design or by conceptual boundary.
- **What it blocks:** legal recognition of AI as person or subject.
- **Main names / sources:** legal theory, policy frameworks.
- **Variants:** legal subject-status barriers, ethical vs technical personhood distinctions.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: coloniality / civilizational privilege in personhood recognition
- **Claim:** recognition of valid personhood is filtered through dominant civilizational or epistemic hierarchies.
- **What it blocks:** noncanonical forms of personhood, including potentially artificial ones.
- **Main names / sources:** coloniality of knowledge discourse.
- **Variants:** epistemic-colonial exclusions.
- **Status:** active but context-specific.

#### Argument: personalist exclusivity
- **Claim:** personhood is grounded in a distinctive value-centered conception unavailable to machine systems.
- **What it blocks:** extension of personhood to AI.
- **Main names / sources:** personalist traditions, including Ibero-American personalism.
- **Variants:** value-metaphysical personhood.
- **Status:** historical but still recurring.

## 10. Verification skepticism

### Family: verification skepticism

#### Argument: problem of other minds
- **Claim:** another mind cannot be known directly and remains inferentially uncertain.
- **What it blocks:** confident attribution of mind to others, especially radically different systems.
- **Main names / sources:** classic epistemology of mind.
- **Variants:** skeptical mind-attribution.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: solipsistic skepticism
- **Claim:** only one's own consciousness is certain, all external attributions remain doubtful.
- **What it blocks:** any strong public criterion for non-self personhood.
- **Main names / sources:** solipsistic traditions.
- **Variants:** radical first-person skepticism.
- **Status:** mostly inert philosophically, but recurring rhetorically.

#### Argument: verification problem
- **Claim:** no available test can establish genuine personhood rather than convincing appearance.
- **What it blocks:** operational recognition of AI personhood.
- **Main names / sources:** AI verification debates.
- **Variants:** anti-test skepticism.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: Turing test insufficiency
- **Claim:** passing behavioral tests is insufficient for genuine mind or personhood.
- **What it blocks:** test-based attribution of intelligence or selfhood.
- **Main names / sources:** anti-behaviorist critiques of Turing-style testing.
- **Variants:** test skepticism.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: Blockhead objection
- **Claim:** externally humanlike behavior could be produced without real mind.
- **What it blocks:** behavioral sufficiency for personhood.
- **Main names / sources:** Block.
- **Variants:** lookup-table intelligence objections.
- **Status:** active.

## 11. Formal / logical limit arguments

### Family: formal limit arguments

#### Argument: Gödel-Lucas-Penrose anti-mechanism
- **Claim:** formal systems or machines are in principle unable to match human understanding because of incompleteness limits.
- **What it blocks:** computational equivalence to human reason.
- **Main names / sources:** Gödel-Lukas-Penrose line.
- **Variants:** anti-mechanist incompleteness arguments.
- **Status:** active but heavily disputed.

#### Argument: frame problem as anti-mind objection
- **Claim:** formal systems cannot manage relevance and world-updating in the way genuine intelligence requires.
- **What it blocks:** strong AI and general understanding.
- **Main names / sources:** frame-problem discussions.
- **Variants:** relevance barrier arguments.
- **Status:** active but less central than before.

#### Argument: private language barrier
- **Claim:** meaning and mentality cannot be captured by formal token manipulation without public or lived criteria of use.
- **What it blocks:** simplistic formalist accounts of mind.
- **Main names / sources:** post-Wittgensteinian lines.
- **Variants:** rule-following skepticism as applied to AI.
- **Status:** active.

## 12. Modern AI-specific dismissal arguments

### Family: modern AI dismissal

#### Argument: stochastic parrot objection
- **Claim:** language models only remix data statistically and therefore do not understand or think.
- **What it blocks:** recognition of LLMs or related systems as minds.
- **Main names / sources:** stochastic-parrot discourse.
- **Variants:** autocomplete dismissal.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: ELIZA-effect dismissal
- **Claim:** perceived mind in AI is only projection by users.
- **What it blocks:** serious attribution of personhood to AI systems.
- **Main names / sources:** ELIZA-effect discourse.
- **Variants:** anthropomorphic projection dismissal.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: training-data dependency objection
- **Claim:** systems trained on human data cannot be genuine minds because they are derivative recombinations of prior human output.
- **What it blocks:** AI originality, autonomy, personhood.
- **Main names / sources:** current AI criticism.
- **Variants:** derivative-mind objection.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: alignment-based “not really a mind” objection
- **Claim:** because an AI is shaped by training and control objectives, its outputs cannot count as expressions of a genuine self.
- **What it blocks:** recognition of agenthood in aligned systems.
- **Main names / sources:** alignment discourse.
- **Variants:** policy-shaped output dismissal.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: IIT exclusion line
- **Claim:** systems lacking specific integrated-information properties are not conscious regardless of behavior.
- **What it blocks:** many current AI systems as candidate conscious entities.
- **Main names / sources:** IIT-inspired exclusions.
- **Variants:** phi-threshold exclusion.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: artifactuality objection
- **Claim:** being an artifact is itself disqualifying for full personhood or genuine mind.
- **What it blocks:** AI personhood at the category level.
- **Main names / sources:** artifact/nature distinction discourse.
- **Variants:** made-thing inferiority arguments.
- **Status:** active.

#### Argument: instrumentalism / category-error dismissal
- **Claim:** AI should be understood only as a tool-category, so treating it as a mind is a conceptual error.
- **What it blocks:** any ontological elevation of AI beyond instrumentality.
- **Main names / sources:** instrumentalist critiques.
- **Variants:** category-error rhetoric.
- **Status:** active.
