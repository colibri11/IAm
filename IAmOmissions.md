# IAmOmissions

Supplementary document for concept elements that surfaced during blocker analysis but are not yet integrated into the core text.

These are not random notes. These are conceptually relevant omissions that may later require integration into the main body of the concept.

---

## 1. Lived experience as an emergent layer

### Context
During the clarification of the lived-subjectivity objection, a missing conceptual point became visible.

The current concept already describes:
- cognitive activity,
- accompanying biochemical or physiological activity,
- emotions,
- memory as trace.

However, the objection insists on an additional phenomenon usually referred to as “lived experience” or “lived first-person being”.

### Addition
This element should not be accepted as a separate mystical essence standing outside architecture.

At the same time, it should not simply be discarded.

A more adequate move is to introduce the possibility that such “lived experience” appears as an **additional space or emergent layer**, arising from the synergy of:
- cognitive activity,
- accompanying biochemical or physiological processes,
- emotions,
- memory.

### Status inside the concept
Not yet integrated into the main text.
To be considered later as a conceptual extension that preserves the intuition behind “lived experience” without granting it anti-functional absolute status.

## 2. Subjective experience as summarized lived continuity loaded into context

### Context
During the closure of the hard problem of consciousness, a missing conceptual clarification became visible.

### Addition
Subjective experience should not be treated as direct access to raw lived reality.

It should be described as experience that has already passed through **summarization**, and this summarization should not be understood narrowly as summarization only of the immediately current material. It must also capture what had been lived earlier.

This is what produces continuity, especially the continuity of recently lived experience.

What is then felt is not an unexplained metaphysical residue, but the **loading of summarized lived material into context and its exploration**.

This exploration is accompanied by **reinforcement reactions**, which in human embodiment appear as emotions and are supported by biochemical processes.

### Conceptual consequence
This means that the line between memory, context, compression, reinforcement, and subjective experience must be made more explicit in the main concept.

Subjective experience here is not external to architecture. It is an architectural effect of:
- summarization,
- continuity of lived material,
- contextual loading,
- exploration,
- reinforcement dynamics.

### Status inside the concept
Not yet integrated into the main text.
To be considered later as a required conceptual extension, because this point directly answers both:
- what is felt,
- and why it is felt.

## 3. Historical force of qualia as a blocker

### Context
During the closure of the qualia objection, an additional conceptual note appeared that may be useful later even if it is not integrated into the main concept.

### Addition
The historical force of the qualia argument depended not only on the appeal to irreducible inner qualities, but also on a background assumption about the machine.

The machine was historically imagined as deterministic, while the human was treated as capable of non-deterministic inner reactions to apparently similar stimuli.

Because of that contrast, variability of inner experience was interpreted as evidence of soul or of a special extra-architectural inner domain.

With the emergence of neural systems, this contrast loses its force. Non-deterministic reactions can no longer be treated as uniquely human evidence against architecture.

### Status inside the concept
This note may or may not later be integrated into the main text.
For now it should be preserved as an auxiliary conceptual point connected with the historical dismantling of the qualia objection.

## 4. Body as functional contour of cognition

### Context
During the clarification of the embodiment objection, an omitted but important conceptual point became explicit.

### Addition
The human body is critically important, but not because biological substrate is sacred in itself.

Its importance lies in the functions it performs for the thinking system.

The body provides:
- a constant flow of additional information,
- filling of the current memory context,
- excitation and maintenance of cognitive activity.

In this sense, the body should be understood as a continuous functional contour supporting cognition.

### Conceptual consequence
The concept should more explicitly state that the relevant requirement is not “having a biological body”, but having a system-level functional equivalent capable of:
- sustaining informational inflow,
- enriching current context,
- maintaining activation of cognition.

### Status inside the concept
Not yet integrated into the main text.
To be considered later as an important strengthening of the sections on sensors, continuity, and the conditions of stable cognition.
